Reading the numbers

why min craters here (and didn't at 4K)

At 1080p the min falls to 0–2.5 on every rung — even 2160p bottoms out near 2.5 — where the 4K view held ~20. The 1080p reference is a pristine supersampled image, so a rung's real compression artifacts on the hardest frames (fast spray, high texture) show up as sharp, localized errors → a few frames score near zero. At 4K those same frames are blurred by the upscale into an even softness, so nothing craters. Same encodes; 1080p is the microscope, 4K is the billboard.

the harmonic plateau — bitrate above 1080p doesn't fix the worst frames

From 1080p to 2160p the mean climbs 92.1 → 97.2, but the harmonic barely moves — 83.8 → 88.4 — and the min stays ~2.5 the whole way. The average improves because most frames get crisper, but the handful of genuinely hard frames are artifact/content-limited, not resolution-limited: no amount of extra bitrate on a premium rung rescues them. If your quality bar is "worst moments," the top rungs buy you little.

the crossover, explained

Low rungs score worse at 1080p (234p: 13.3 vs 24.7 at 4K) because their blockiness is fully exposed against a sharp reference. High rungs score better at 1080p (720p: 85.7 vs 78.5) because they're at/near native scale — little upscaling, no resolution penalty. The lines cross around 400–600 kbps. Neither ranking is "wrong": grade at 1080p to tune compression for HD delivery, at 4K to judge how a rung reads on a 4K screen.

234p at native scale

234p is nearly unwatchable here — mean 13.3, 43.7% of frames below VMAF 10, 99.5% below 50. Against a crisp 1080p reference the 138 kbps floor has nowhere to hide. It's a bandwidth-survival rung, full stop.
