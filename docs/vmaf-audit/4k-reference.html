Reading the numbers

mean vs harmonic

Mean averages every frame equally; harmonic mean is dominated by the lowest-scoring frames (it's n / Σ(1/score)), so it reads as "how bad do the bad moments make it feel." At 4K the two sit close — never more than ~3 apart — which tells you the degradation is uniform, not spiky. There's no frame that craters; the rung is just consistently soft relative to the 4K master.

why min never craters at 4K

The min holds around 19–21 for every rung 360p and up. Upscaling a lower rung to 4K blurs its compression artifacts into an even softness rather than sharp errors — blurry-but-even beats sharp-but-wrong, so no single frame is catastrophically off. The 1080p-reference report (next) grades the rungs at their own scale, where real compression artifacts surface and the min drops toward 0 on the hardest frames. Same encodes, different question: 4K asks "how much of the 4K experience does it deliver," 1080p asks "how clean is the compression."

the ~t300s dip is content, not a rung problem

Every rung's timeline dips at the same window (~t300s → the mid-70s even on 2160p). It's a genuinely hard stretch of the clip — fast water spray, high texture — that no rung fully resolves. Because it hits all rungs together, it's real content difficulty, and the vertical gap between rungs there is exactly how much bitrate buys you on the hardest content.

%<50 as a reach signal

At 4K, 234p has 96.6% of frames below VMAF 50 and 360p 88.9% — these low rungs look poor on a 4K display, as expected: they exist to keep playback alive under collapsed bandwidth, not to look good. By 684p only ~3.6% of frames fall below 50, and it flattens from there.
