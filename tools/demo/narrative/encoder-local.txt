[00] Infinite Streaming Encoder. An ffmpeg parallel encoder farm using local networked machines or AWS cloud.
[01] The Originals tab lists the source files on the master.
[02] Selecting a five and a half minute 4K FPV clip.
[03] These are the encoding options. Everything that decides what gets produced, and where it runs.
[04] Here is what each control does.
[05] Target. Where the encode runs.
[06] Selecting Cloud. Submits the job to AWS Batch on spot capacity. Costs money, and adds controls for spot, leaving media in S3, and deferred packaging. The estimate switches from time to money. About $1.59 for this clip.
[07] Returning to a local encode. And the estimate is time again.
[08] Chunking. How each variant is split into pieces that encode in parallel.
[09] Selecting Dynamic. Sizes each variant from its learnt encode speed, aiming at ~4 minutes of work per chunk — so a cheap rung is one long chunk, 4K HEVC is many short ones, and they finish together. Cloud sizes for Graviton; local sizes for the slowest box on the farm, since any worker can pick up any chunk.
[10] Selecting 12 second chunks. Maximum parallelism. Also the most chunk boundaries, each one a queue wait, a container start and an encoder reset.
[11] Codec. Which encoders to run. Each codec is a separate job with its own output directory, so two codecs is two jobs, not one job twice as long.
[12] Ladder / Profile. A ladder is the resolutions and bitrates and the delivery timing that goes with them — segment, partial, GOP and VBV. Pick several to encode one source as several delivery profiles, one job each.
[13] Max Resolution. Drops ladder rungs taller than this. The tiers listed come from the selected ladder's real rung heights, not a fixed set. Min Resolution. Drops ladder rungs shorter than this. With Max Resolution it selects a contiguous band of the ladder rather than a top or a bottom.
[14] Duration. Encode only the first N seconds, snapped to a whole segment so chunk boundaries still land on segment edges. Blank encodes the whole clip, and so does any value at or above the clip's own length.
[15] Output suffix. Appended to the output directory name, after the codec. Set one to keep comparison encodes of the same source side by side instead of each archiving the last.
[16] Shared decode. Encodes the decode-dominated rungs — 1080p and below — in bands of three that share one decode per chunk instead of one ffmpeg each. Local target only. The ladder and every rung's settings are unchanged, so the output is the same.
[17] Burn-in text overlay. Burns timecode, bitrate, codec, resolution, encoder and a watermark into every variant, so a frame says what produced it. On by default; uncheck for clean output.
[18] Promote after encode. Rsyncs each finished output to the live locations as soon as the job completes, instead of pressing Promote per output afterwards. Best-effort: a failed copy is logged and does not fail the encode.
[19] First, the settings for this run.
[20] Codec: H.264, which is what the form starts with. Every codec is its own job, so this run is one job.
[21] The ladder is apple-uniq-live-xs. It carries the rungs and the delivery profile together.
[22] Before running it — this is the ladder we are about to encode with.
[23] apple-uniq-live-xs. One object holding both halves: the rungs, and the delivery profile they are encoded to.
[24] Its delivery profile: the VBV shaping, the segment and partial lengths, the GOP, and the suffix. A ladder that pins its segment length is tagged with that length and served exactly as encoded. This one pins nothing and is tagged _xs — the flexible base, one encode that go-live re-chops into one, two and six second segments.
[25] The rungs, 14 of them. Each one's resolution and target bitrate, and the step up from the rung below. Colour marks a step too small for the rung to earn its place, or a jump big enough to leave a hole.
[26] And the shaping. Each bar runs from average to peak bitrate over a one, two and six second window, against Apple's limits — peak within one and a quarter times average for live, twice that for video on demand. This profile sits right on the live limit even at a one second chop. A ladder pinned to six seconds sits on the same limit at six — but chop it to one and its peak doubles. That is the whole reason only this ladder may be re-chopped.
